A website is useful when a visitor can understand what you offer, decide whether it fits their needs, and take the next step without guessing.

## Answer the first questions clearly

The opening section should help a visitor understand your organisation. Explain what you do in familiar language. Describe who you help and what they can do next.

Avoid making someone read several sections before discovering the service they came for.

## Give each page a purpose

Your services page can explain what a project might include. Your work page can show relevant examples. Your contact page can make it easy to start a conversation.

1. Decide what the visitor needs to know.
2. Present the evidence you actually have.
3. Provide a clear, working next step.

## Make the small-screen experience complete

A visitor on a phone needs the same core information as a visitor at a desk. Keep text readable, links easy to select, and forms short enough to understand.

<Callout>
  Try your website as a new visitor: find a service, review an example, and start an enquiry. Any hesitation is useful feedback.
</Callout>

## Keep content accurate

Use real project information and current contact details. If an example is a prototype, say so. If a feature is not ready, explain what a visitor can do instead.

Good presentation helps people notice your work. Accurate information helps them decide what to do with it.
